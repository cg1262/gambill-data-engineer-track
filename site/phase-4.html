W44 Architecture decision-making — design & defend your capstone M32 · Phase 4 · launch G9G1
Why this matters You are the founding data engineer for a company in your target industry. Leadership has funded exactly one data product and wants to know, before a single line of code is written, what it will be, who decides differently because of it, and why your architecture is the right bet — because rework after go-live is expensive and your job is on the line. This week you earn the right to build by designing something you can defend to a skeptical senior in 30 seconds.
Launch · this week Launch gate for the solo capstone: this is the design-before-code week where the learner locks their own industry, validates a real messy source, and commits the charter + 4-part architecture + 3 ADRs that every subsequent capstone-build week (W46+) executes against. Getting this right is what makes the later build fast and the final GitHub read as senior.
Objectives
  • Write a one-page capstone project charter that names the problem, the decision-making persona, the data-product grain, and 3 measurable KPIs — such that a reviewer can restate the business problem in one sentence without reading the code.
  • Produce a 4-part architecture document (context/C4-lite, medallion data flow with grain per layer, governance & security model, deployment/CI-CD topology) that a senior reviewer can trace end-to-end from source to golden record.
  • Author 3 Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) for pivotal choices (e.g., batch vs streaming, star vs Data Vault, exact vs fuzzy matching) that each state the context, the options considered, what was given up, and the revisit trigger.
  • Validate the chosen real public source by profiling it (volume, refresh cadence, messiness, licence/ToS) and defending in writing why it is rich enough to reach golden records.
  • Defend the design verbally in a trade-off shape (‘I chose X over Y because Z, which cost me A, and I would revisit via B’) tied to a named persona, not a tool.
Concept lessons · ~4h
  • Design before code: why the charter comes first (‘slow on the front end, fast on the back end’). Grain, persona, and KPI as the three things that make a data product defensible. Recognition before evaluation — the 1–2 sentence business framing that opens every artifact. (0.75 hr)
  • The 4-part architecture doc as a senior signal: (1) context/C4-lite system diagram, (2) medallion data flow with the declared grain at bronze/silver/gold, (3) Unity Catalog governance & security model, (4) DABs/CI-CD deployment topology. What a 30-second GitHub skim must reveal. (1.0 hr)
  • Architecture Decision Records: the Nygard format (context → decision → status → consequences). Writing the option you REJECTED as carefully as the one you chose; the ‘what I gave up’ and the ‘revisit trigger’ as interview gold. (1.0 hr)
  • The three pivotal capstone decisions in depth: batch vs micro-batch/streaming; dimensional star vs Data Vault vs one-big-table; exact vs fuzzy (blocking + similarity) entity matching for MDM. Cost, latency, and maintainability trade-offs of each. (0.75 hr)
  • Source selection & feasibility: profiling a real public source for volume, refresh cadence, natural keys, duplication/messiness, and licence/ToS before you commit 6 weeks to it. Red flags that kill a capstone source. (0.5 hr)
Hands-on lab · ~6h

A pure design-before-code week: you stand up your capstone repository skeleton and fill it with decision artifacts — a project charter, a 4-part architecture document, and 3 ADRs — anchored to a real, profiled public source in your own target industry. No pipeline code ships this week; the deliverable is a defensible design a reviewer can trace from source to golden record. You are proving a human made judgment calls and wrote down why.

Step by step
  1. Pick your industry and shortlist 2 candidate real public sources (from data.gov, SEC EDGAR, CoinGecko, ReliefWeb, NOAA, CMS/HealthData.gov, or Faker for locked-down/fraud domains). For each, open the actual portal and note volume, refresh cadence, format, and licence/ToS.
  2. Profile your top candidate for 30–60 min: pull a sample, count rows, inspect natural keys, spot duplicates/nulls/format drift, and confirm it is messy enough and rich enough to reach golden records. Write a 5-line ‘source feasibility’ note and commit the sample profile.
  3. Scaffold the capstone repo from the starter template: create /docs (charter.md, architecture.md), /docs/adr (0001–0003), /src, /tests, /resources placeholders, and a README stub. Do NOT write pipeline code — leave TODO markers.
  4. Write charter.md: 1–2 sentence business framing, the problem, the named decision-making persona, the data-product grain (one row = ?), and 3 measurable KPIs the product moves. Restate the problem in one sentence at the top.
  5. Write architecture.md Part 1 (context/C4-lite diagram: source → Databricks → consumer) and Part 2 (medallion data flow) — declare the explicit grain at bronze, silver, and gold, and where CDC/idempotency and quarantine live.
  6. Write architecture.md Part 3 (Unity Catalog governance: catalogs/schemas, ownership, access grants, PII handling) and Part 4 (deployment: DABs bundle layout, CI-CD stages, environments).
  7. Author 3 ADRs using the template — one each for a batch-vs-streaming choice, a modeling choice (star vs Data Vault vs OBT), and a matching choice (exact vs fuzzy). In each, record options considered, the decision, what you gave up, and the revisit trigger.
  8. Do a ‘30-second skim’ self-audit: open only the README + top of each doc and check a stranger could name the problem, the grain, and the 3 decisions. Fix anything that fails the skim.
  9. Record a 3–5 minute Loom walking a reviewer through the charter and one ADR in trade-off shape (‘I chose X over Y because Z, which cost me A, I’d revisit via B’), tying the decision to your persona. Commit and push everything with a clean history.

Concept lessons · ~7.5h
  • Why an elective, and how to choose (0.5 hr): 'add one advanced capability, not five.' Over-engineering is the trap this week is designed to expose. Read the two track summaries, then write your choice + reader-first rationale BEFORE any code (recognition before evaluation).
  • TRACK A concepts — Data Vault 2.0 core (2.0 hr): hubs (business keys), links (relationships/transactions), satellites (context + history), hash keys vs. sequence keys, load_date and record_source as first-class lineage, insert-only/idempotent loads. Where it sits relative to your medallion: raw vault in silver, business vault + star-schema marts in gold.
  • TRACK A concepts — when Data Vault beats a star (1.0 hr): many source systems, changing relationships, hard auditability/regulatory lineage, parallel loads. The cost: more joins, more objects, a mart layer still needed for consumers. Star schema remains right for a single stable source and BI-first grain. Decide with your capstone in hand.
  • TRACK B concepts — LLMs/agents in the pipeline (2.0 hr): the ONLY safe pattern is a narrow, bounded step behind a deterministic contract (typed output, allowed-value set). Semantic/context layer = the curated facts + schema + business glossary you feed the model so it answers about YOUR data, not the internet. Cost, latency, and non-determinism as engineering constraints.
  • TRACK B concepts — guardrails, evals, human-in-the-loop (1.5 hr): input validation + output schema enforcement + confidence/abstain path + quarantine on low confidence (same quarantine discipline as Phases 1-3). Build a labeled gold set, measure pass rate/precision, and log every call for audit. 'The human in the loop' = you own the eval bar and the override.
  • Both tracks — interview framing (0.5 hr): how to tell this as a trade-off story ('why not what'), and why 'I deliberately did NOT do X' is a senior signal on a 30-second skim.

A mini-project in your chosen track, applied to the capstone where possible. TRACK A: re-model one subject area of your capstone (or Project 1) as a Data Vault 2.0 raw vault — hubs, links, satellites with hash keys and lineage columns — load it idempotently in Databricks, then build a small star-schema mart on top and write the ADR on when this earns its keep. Step by step
  1. CHOOSE (both tracks): In your capstone repo, create docs/adr/ADR-00X-elective.md. Write your track choice and a reader-first rationale (2-4 sentences). State what you are deliberately NOT building this week and why (over-engineering guard). Commit before writing any code.
  2. TRACK A — Step 1: Pick ONE subject area from your capstone/Project-1 silver model (e.g., provider–payer–price, or customer–order–product). Sketch the vault on paper: identify business keys (hubs), relationships/transactions (links), and descriptive/historical attributes (satellites). Save the sketch as an image in docs/.
  3. TRACK A — Step 2: In a Databricks notebook, build the hub tables: compute a deterministic hash key over the business key, add load_date and record_source. Load insert-only and idempotent (re-running adds no duplicates). Repeat for link tables (hash of the parent business keys).
  4. TRACK A — Step 3: Build satellite tables: hash-diff the descriptive columns so only CHANGED context inserts a new row (recoverable history). Verify by loading the same batch twice and a modified batch once; confirm row counts prove idempotency + change capture.

  6. TRACK B — Step 1: Pick ONE narrow, bounded task on your real source (standardize free-text categories, extract fields from unstructured text, OR classify records into an allowed set). Define a DETERMINISTIC contract: exact output schema + the closed set of allowed values. Write it down first.

  8. TRACK B — Step 3: Add guardrails: validate/normalize input, enforce the output schema, and route low-confidence or off-contract responses to a QUARANTINE table (reuse your Phase 1-3 quarantine pattern) instead of silently accepting them. Log every call (input, output, confidence) for audit.
  9. TRACK B — Step 4: Hand-label a gold set (30-100 rows), run the eval harness (MLflow evaluate or a simple accuracy/precision script), record the pass rate, and pick ONE case where you override the model — document it. Write the ADR: why a bounded LLM step here, cost/latency/non-determinism, and your revisit trigger.

Step by step
  1. WEEK 1 - DESIGN & CHARTER LOCK (design before code). Finalize the project charter: name the stakeholder, the one business question, the grain of your gold product, the definition of 'done', and explicit non-goals.   2. WEEK 1 - SOURCE & BRONZE. Ingest your real messy source (data.gov / CMS / SEC EDGAR / CoinGecko / ReliefWeb / NOAA / Faker for locked-down domains) into a bronze table exactly as received: full fidelity, source metadata columns (ingest timestamp, source file/endpoint, batch id), no cleaning. Profile the raw data and write down, in the README, the three messiest things you found (nulls, dupes, drifting schema, encoding, late records) - these become your silver requirements.
  3. WEEK 1 - SILVER, TESTED. Build silver: typed, deduplicated, conformed, with a clear primary key at the declared grain. Encode your data-quality expectations as explicit assertions and route failures to a quarantine table with a written reason code - do not silently drop rows. Add the first round of tests (schema, not-null on keys, uniqueness at grain, referential checks). Prove a deliberately corrupted batch lands in quarantine and does NOT reach silver.
  4. WEEK 2 - GOLD PRODUCT. Model the gold layer to answer the charter's business question at the declared grain (dimensional or wide aggregate as fits). Write the grain statement at the top of the gold table doc. Validate gold against a hand-computed spot check so you can defend the numbers, not just produce them.
  5. WEEK 2 - CDC / INCREMENTAL + IDEMPOTENCY PROOF. Convert the load from full-refresh to incremental (MERGE / change-feed / watermark as appropriate). Then PROVE idempotency: run the identical incremental load twice and capture evidence that gold row counts and keys are unchanged. Replay one prior day/batch and show the pipeline reconstructs the same state (recoverable history). Log both proofs as assertions or screenshots in the repo.
  6. WEEK 2 - MDM GOLDEN RECORDS. Resolve entities across your source(s) into golden records: define match rules, survivorship rules (which field wins and why), and a stable golden key. Document why you chose deterministic vs probabilistic matching and what a wrong merge would cost the stakeholder. Show a before/after on a real duplicate cluster from your data.
  7. WEEK 3 - GOVERNANCE & SECURITY. Put the whole product under Unity Catalog: catalog/schema layout, table + column comments, ownership/grants that reflect least privilege, and lineage you can screenshot. Tag or mask at least one sensitive column and write one sentence on the governed-access decision and who it protects.
  8. WEEK 3 - DABs / CI-CD HARDENING. Package everything as a Databricks Asset Bundle and deploy through a CI/CD pipeline (lint + tests run on push, bundle deploys the job). Prove repeatable deployment: tear the job down and redeploy it from a clean clone with one command, and confirm it runs green. This is the 'repeatable deployment' pillar made real.
  9. WEEK 3 - RECORD, POLISH & DEFEND. Finalize the README for the 30-second senior skim (grain, diagram, run instructions, test/CI badges, ADR links). Record a 5-8 minute walkthrough that opens on the business question and ends on golden records. Pre-write trade-off-shaped answers to the three hardest questions about your build.
