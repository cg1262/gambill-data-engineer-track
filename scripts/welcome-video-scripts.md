# Welcome Video Scripts — Gambill Data Engineer Track

Three short welcome scripts, one per path, for Chris to record to camera.
Target ~60–90s each. Voice: direct, warm, second person, no hype.

---

## 1. Launch — "Start From Zero"
**Target:** ~80 seconds (~195 words) · beginner, no experience

So you've maybe got some Excel under your belt, and that's about it. Good. You're exactly who this is for.

Here's the thing. Data engineering isn't about writing pipelines. It's about building data products — things you can stand behind when someone asks you a hard question. And nobody's born knowing how to do that. You learn it by building.

Launch is the full road. Four phases — Foundations, Core, Platform and Production, then Advanced — about eleven, twelve months, self-paced, with one live session every week. Plan on eight to twelve hours. That's real, but it's doable alongside a life.

We don't use clean tutorial data. We use the messy public stuff, the kind that fights back — because that's the job. And every week you don't just build. You defend your decisions, out loud, in a live review. That's the whole game.

By the end you've got a portfolio that lands on golden records and MDM. The kind of project that makes a hiring manager lean in and go, "wait, we have this exact problem."

Start at the beginning. Come build something you can defend.

## 2. Bridge — "You Already Know Half of This"
**Target:** ~75 seconds (~185 words) · analysts & SWEs crossing over

Let's be honest — you can already write code, or you already live in the data. You just don't carry the title "data engineer" yet. I'm not going to make you sit through things you already own.

Bridge is for analysts and software engineers crossing over. Three to five months. It starts with a placement diagnostic — one honest look at what you've got — and routes you to the analyst track or the SWE track. You skip the stuff you know. You go deep on the gaps.

And look, the gaps are different depending on where you come from. Analysts, it's usually the engineering rigor. Engineers, it's usually modeling and thinking in data products. Either way, we converge on the same place — the lakehouse core and a real capstone.

Same rhythm as everything we do: self-paced, one live session a week, built on real messy data. And every week ends with you defending your decisions on the hot seat. Because knowing it and being able to defend it are two different things.

You walk out with an MDM-grade portfolio that says "data engineer" without you having to.

Take the diagnostic. Let's find your gaps.

## 3. Sharpen — "Fill the Gaps You Actually Have"
**Target:** ~70 seconds (~170 words) · working DEs, modular

You're already doing this job. Shipping pipelines, on call, the whole thing. But there are a few things that slipped past you along the way — and you know exactly the feeling I'm talking about.

Sharpen is targeted. No fluff, no starting over. It opens with a self-diagnostic against ten gaps that trip up working engineers — the ones nobody quite taught you. Then eight pre-built module bundles, and every single unit stands alone. You take what you need and nothing you don't.

Here's the thing about being busy. You can't afford a course that wastes your time. So this one doesn't. Want just the MDM and golden-records bundle? Take that. Want to tighten up idempotency and quarantine patterns? Take that.

Still self-paced, still a weekly live review if you want the pressure-test — because defending a decision out loud is how you find out if you actually understand it. Does that make sense?

You come out sharper where it counts, with work you can point to.

Run the diagnostic. See what's missing. Fix it.
